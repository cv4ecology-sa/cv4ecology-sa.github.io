I. About

Making computer vision more accessible for ecology.

Computer vision (CV) is transforming ecological research by automating the analysis of data collected from camera traps, drones, satellites and recording devices. While ecologists often have training in statistics, they are rarely exposed to the AI and CV skills necessary to analyze large volumes of visual data on their own.

But access to educational resources to teach these essential skills is uneven; most courses are offered in OECD countries and geared toward practitioners with substantial financial resources. As a result, scientists and conservation practitioners in the Global South—despite working in critical biodiversity-rich regions—often face barriers to analysing their own data using these emerging methods, reinforcing long-standing inequities in conservation science and limiting locally driven decision-making.

Workshop format

Five days of practical learning, discussion and collaboration.

The workshop will combine lectures, practical sessions and ecological case studies, with a focus on applying computer vision to real-world research and conservation challenges.
